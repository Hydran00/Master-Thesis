\chapter{Introduction}

Telehealth is commonly defined as the provision of healthcare services through remote means, obviating the necessity for in-person visits.
This paradigm is notably efficacious in reaching geographically isolated areas and has experienced increased adoption during the COVID-19 pandemic, primarily due to its inherent capacity to mitigate the risk of pathogen transmission between patients and healthcare providers.
Telerobotics, a promising advancement, involves remote-controlled robotic systems interacting with the environment, integrating tools like virtual reality\cite{meng2023virtual}, haptic interfaces\cite{nahri2021review}, and precise control\cite{ruwanthika2020precise}. Among various applications, remote ultrasound examination enhances patient comfort and reduces costs for both patients and the healthcare system.~\cite{eu2018market,who2022consolidated}
Ultrasound imaging, utilising high-frequency sound waves for noninvasive evaluation of internal structures, provides a safe diagnostic method for detecting and monitoring diseases. However, it necessitates skilled sonographers and risks work-related musculoskeletal disorders due to exerted force~\cite{coffin2014work}. The quality of the diagnosis very much depends on the skills of the sonographer, and the number of experienced operators is not sufficient to deliver the service in remote areas~\cite{li2021overview}.

\section{Literature Review}

Remote robotised lung ultrasound (RUSS), shows significant promise with recent successful implementations~\cite{avgousti2016medical,jiang2023robotic} addressing those issues. 
In the direction of a more objective way of collecting data from patients, researchers have also focused on developing standardised and clinically validated procedures~\cite{Soldati2020ProposalCOVID-19,Demi2023NewUltrasound} for lung ultrasound.
Literature provides both autonomous~\cite{su2024fully,li2021overview,roshan2022robotic} and 
teleoperated~\cite{vonhaxthausen2021medical,jiang2023robotic} solutions for RUSS. The latest relevant work with respect to fully autonomous frameworks is proposed by \textit{Su et al.}  \cite{su2024fully} in which the authors proposed a complete pipeline for the ultrasound scanning of the thyroid. A rough planning algorithm that guides the robot to the interest region is computed exploiting the point cloud of the patient measured from a RGB-D camera. One of the problems due to this approaches is the significant anatomical variability across individuals, making fully autonomous systems not yet ready for widespread implementation in hospitals and healthcare facilities.

\noindent Telepresence and teleoperated robotic systems, instead, have proven to be effective solutions, especially in cases where human decision-making capabilities are still superior to the level of autonomy reached by the intelligence of such systems.
% In medical applications, for example, because of the high variability induced by the anatomy of the human body, autonomous solutions are still far from populating hospitals and health centres. 
However, physical telehealth systems require trained experts capable of not only operating remotely machines but also replicating complex medical procedures. Due to these constraints, the number of experts is usually limited and below demand. For this reason, user-friendliness and the ability to provide reliable feedback~\cite{fu2023robot}, assist~\cite{huang2024robot-assisted} and train~\cite{shahbazi2013Dual-user} the operator are of paramount importance.
Virtual fixtures (VFs) are widely used tools to provide assistance to a remote operator in a shared control fashion, by constraining a teleoperated robot to stay within/avoid certain regions in space~\cite{bowyer2014active}.
Although initial approaches envisioned the prior computations of such regions, which is not always feasible, more recent ones tried to exploit visual data~\cite{bettini2004vision,moccia2020vision-based} and point clouds~\cite{ryden2012forbidden} to initialise them. However, these methods assume that the constrained region is always directly measurable and can be sensed with cameras.

\noindent In lung ultrasound, the probe should be placed in the intercostal areas~\cite{Soldati2020ProposalCOVID-19,Demi2023NewUltrasound} to avoid the shadowing effect due to the presence of the ribs~\cite{ogul2016eliminating}. Detecting the bones and thus placing the probe, mounted on the end effector of the manipulator, in such a narrow area could become a complex task, especially in the case of teleoperated robotic ultrasound exams, where standard visual and haptic feedback, fundamental in in-person exams, might not suffice. 

\noindent One of the main issues arises when the robot is in close proximity to the patient. In such a case, the patient's view captured by a fixed RGB camera might be occluded by the robot. If the camera is mounted on the robot's end effector, instead, it might be too close to the patient with a limited field of view. In such a case, a dynamic 3D model, which includes the anatomical features of the patient, such as the rib cage in the case of lung ultrasound, and the target pose of the robot might provide great support. Also, when the probe is in contact with the body, with standard haptic feedback, it is not easy to distinguish if the probe is located on top of a bone or a soft tissue and thus the practitioner should rely mainly on the ultrasound image.
Another issue is represented by the low repeatability and the high subjectivity of the operations, as the success of the exam depends very much on the practitioner's ability.

\section{Contribution}

\noindent To overcome these limitations and assist the physician in the teleoperation tasks, we present in this manuscript an anatomy-aware shared control framework for lung ultrasound examination, which leverages novel biomechanically accurate 3D digital models, such as SMPL~\cite{loper2015smpl} and SKEL~\cite{keller2023skel} to generate reliable 3D visual feedback for the human operator and define the end-effector forbidden regions that are enforced by the presence of VFs. In this way, the physician is assisted in two ways: \begin{enumerate}
    \item in wide robot motions, such as the reach of the target areas, through the anatomical 3D visual feedback, which ease the task of finding the desired probe placement, and
    \item in the fine-grained placements on the intercostal area, thanks to the presence of the VFs, which forbids the probe to end up on the ribs.
\end{enumerate}
The framework consists of two main components: the generation of the patient's 3D virtual body model and the enforcement of the VFs on the reference coming from the haptic device controlled by the physician. In the first part, the coloured point cloud of the patient is filtered thanks to the accurate human segmentation provided by YOLOv8-seg~\cite{jocher2023yolov8} and then fitted through regression to a SMPL model that approximates the volumetric occupancy of the body of the patient. Based on the SMPL model, a biomechanical skeletal model (SKEL) is generated. The rib cage is then projected on the SMPL to define the forbidden regions. The resulting mesh is passed to a constraint programme that ensures that the reference position of the haptic interface stays out of the VFs. The filtered position is then transmitted to the interaction controller, which regulates the motion of the robot. The force and torque exerted by the robot on the environment, measured at the end effector, are transmitted back to the haptic interface, providing the physician with tactile feedback to indicate contact with the patient's body.

\noindent The proposed framework is evaluated on a proof-of concept teleoperated lung ultrasound examination procedure. We performed the exam on male healthy subjects, and we assessed three different aspects:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item the precision and accuracy of the SMPL to model the patient's body geometry with respect to anthropomorphic measurements;
    \item the capability of the VFs to assist the teleoperation in the probe placement within the intercostal areas;
    \item the efficiency of the framework in performing the protocol in terms of duration, compared to a standard teleoperation without assistance.
\end{enumerate}
The results demonstrate the reliability of the anatomical model and highlight that the provided support for the teleoperation is fundamental for an effective remote examination.
